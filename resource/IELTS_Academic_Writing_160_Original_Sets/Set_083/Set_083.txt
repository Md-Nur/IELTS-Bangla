IELTS ACADEMIC WRITING PRACTICE — SET 083
Unofficial original practice set | Total time: 60 minutes
Task 1: about 20 minutes, minimum 150 words. Task 2: about 40 minutes, minimum 250 words.

TASK 1 — ACADEMIC REPORT
Spend about 20 minutes on this task. Write at least 150 words.
The diagram illustrates drinking-water treatment.

Stage 1: Raw water enters a screening chamber
Stage 2: Large debris is removed
Stage 3: Coagulant causes fine particles to form larger clusters
Stage 4: Water passes through settling tanks
Stage 5: Sand and carbon filters remove remaining particles
Stage 6: Disinfection is applied before storage and distribution

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features. Describe the sequence and include an overview.

Planning cues: state whether the process is linear or cyclical; group stages logically; use passive forms where appropriate; do not invent quantities or causes.

TASK 2 — ESSAY
Spend about 40 minutes on this task. Write at least 250 words.
In many places, agriculture is becoming more common. Do the advantages of this development outweigh the disadvantages?
Support your answer with reasons and relevant examples. Use full, connected prose rather than notes or bullet points.

Planning challenge:
1. Identify the exact instruction and answer every part of the question.
2. State a clear position in the introduction and maintain it throughout.
3. Develop two main ideas with explanation and specific examples; avoid unsupported generalisations.
4. Consider a counterargument or limitation where relevant.
5. Use a conclusion that follows from the argument rather than introducing a new idea.

IELTS-style self-check:
Task 1: Is there a clear overview? Are the key features selected and comparisons accurate? Are figures and units reported correctly?
Task 2: Have I answered the exact question? Are ideas relevant, extended and supported? Is my position clear?
Coherence and cohesion: Is the order logical? Are paragraphs purposeful and linking natural?
Lexical resource: Is vocabulary precise and appropriate, with controlled collocations and limited repetition?
Grammatical range and accuracy: Are structures varied and accurate, with punctuation supporting meaning?

No single model answer is provided because writing tasks allow multiple defensible responses. Use the prompts and checklist to evaluate your own work.
