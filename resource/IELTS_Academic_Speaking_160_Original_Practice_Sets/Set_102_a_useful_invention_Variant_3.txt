IELTS-STYLE SPEAKING PRACTICE — SET 102
Academic candidate practice | Original practice material

TEST CONDITIONS
- Total speaking-test simulation: 11–14 minutes.
- Part 1: 4–5 minutes. Answer naturally; give a reason or example rather than one-word replies.
- Part 2: 1 minute to prepare, then speak for 1–2 minutes. Use brief notes, not a written script.
- Part 3: 4–5 minutes. Develop opinions with explanations, comparisons, examples and, where appropriate, qualifications.
- Treat this as a timed mock interview. A partner should act as the examiner and should not coach you during answers.

PART 1 — INTRODUCTION AND INTERVIEW (4–5 MINUTES)
Topic area: A useful invention
1. How important is a useful invention in your everyday life?
2. Where did you first become interested in it?
3. Do you prefer doing it alone or with other people?
4. What do you find most enjoyable about it?
5. Is there anything you dislike about it?
6. How does it differ for younger and older people?
7. Do you think it will become more common in the future?
8. What makes a good experience of it?

PART 2 — INDIVIDUAL LONG TURN (3–4 MINUTES INCLUDING PREPARATION)
You have ONE minute to make notes. Then speak for up to TWO minutes.

TASK CARD
Describe a time when an invention that improved everyday life involved a change or challenge.
You should say:
- what the situation was
- why it was challenging or different
- what action you took
and explain what you learned from it.

Planning cue: Describe the situation, what made it difficult or different, and what you learned.
Suggested note structure: context → 2–3 key details → reaction/meaning → brief reflection.
Do not try to memorise a model answer. Personal examples may be real or plausible, but keep them consistent and relevant.

Possible examiner follow-up:
- Would you do anything differently if you experienced this again?

PART 3 — TWO-WAY DISCUSSION (4–5 MINUTES)
Theme: A useful invention — innovation, ethics, and unintended effects
1. Why do some people value a useful invention more than others?
2. How does family background or education influence people's attitudes towards it?
3. Do younger generations approach it differently from older generations?
4. What kinds of inequalities can arise around it?
5. How should schools, businesses or public institutions respond?
6. What changes do you expect in the future, and why?

POST-TEST SELF-REVIEW (not an official band score)
Rate yourself from 1 (needs substantial work) to 5 (strong and consistent):
- Fluency and coherence: __/5
- Lexical resource: __/5
- Grammatical range and accuracy: __/5
- Pronunciation and intelligibility: __/5

Reflection:
1. Where did I hesitate, repeat myself, or lose the thread?
2. Which words or collocations could I have used more precisely?
3. Did I vary sentence structures naturally and accurately?
4. Were my word stress, sentence stress, sounds and intonation clear?
5. In Part 3, did I explain and support my opinions rather than simply state them?
6. What one specific improvement will I practise next time?

IMPORTANT
This is original IELTS-style practice, not an official IELTS test. The questions are not predictions of live questions and the self-review is not an examiner-assigned band score.
