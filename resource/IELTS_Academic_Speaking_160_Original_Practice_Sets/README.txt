IELTS ACADEMIC SPEAKING — 160 ORIGINAL PRACTICE SETS
=====================================================

What is included
- 160 numbered speaking mock sets.
- Every set includes Part 1 interview questions, a Part 2 cue card with bullet prompts, a Part 3 abstract discussion, realistic timing, and a self-review checklist.
- Topic areas cover education, work, daily life, culture, technology, environment, community, transport, choices, and personal experiences.
- The four Part 2 angles (experience, influence, change/challenge, choice/recommendation) create varied practice across the topic areas.
- INDEX.csv lists every set.

How to use the sets
1. Set a timer and have a partner act as examiner if possible.
2. Run Part 1 for 4–5 minutes, Part 2 with exactly one minute of planning and up to two minutes speaking, and Part 3 for 4–5 minutes.
3. Record your practice where appropriate and review it using the four official speaking assessment areas.
4. Practise spontaneous language. Avoid memorising full answers; adapt your examples and opinions to the question.
5. Repeat the same set later and compare clarity, development, fluency and pronunciation.

Important format facts
The IELTS Speaking test is the same for Academic and General Training. It lasts 11–14 minutes and has three parts:
- Part 1: introduction and interview, 4–5 minutes.
- Part 2: individual long turn; one minute to prepare, then speak for up to two minutes; the part lasts 3–4 minutes including preparation.
- Part 3: two-way discussion on more general and abstract issues, 4–5 minutes.

The four assessment criteria are:
1. Fluency and coherence
2. Lexical resource
3. Grammatical range and accuracy
4. Pronunciation

Quality note
These are original practice materials inspired by the broad structure and preparation principles used by reputable IELTS preparation sites. They are not copied from official or commercial question banks, are not official IELTS materials, and are not validated or scored by IELTS examiners. The same broad topic areas and question frameworks recur in a deliberately structured way, while cue-card angle and discussion prompts vary. No model answers are included so learners can practise spontaneous speaking.
